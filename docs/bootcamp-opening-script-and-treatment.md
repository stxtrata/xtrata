# Xtrata Bootcamp — first five minutes

Production draft for Friday, 11 September 2026. Presenter: Jim. Host: TheBigMac / BFF ARMY.

Deliverable: timed spoken script, audiovisual treatment, asset list and playback cues. This is a production plan; stills, animations and videos described below have not yet been produced or captured.

## Creative direction

**A picture. A song. A game. What connects them?**

Open with the experience, then explain the idea. The tone is welcoming, curious and practical. The opening should feel like Jim inviting people into a workshop. Use actual work and readable interfaces rather than stock cryptocurrency imagery.

Master: 1920 × 1080, 16:9, 30 fps. Charcoal background, warm white text, orange highlights and blue connecting lines. Use the existing Xtrata logo without redrawing it. Use approved BFF ARMY branding when supplied. Keep titles to one or two lines; reserve the bottom area for captions. Approximately 580 spoken words, with the remaining time used for music, visual holds and natural pauses. Rehearse to the timecodes; the script alone does not guarantee an exact five-minute delivery.

## Timed script and audiovisual treatment

### 00:00–00:20 — The hook

**Jim, voiceover:**

“A picture. A song. A game.

Different things we make, share, and enjoy online.

Today, I want to show you what happens when we put them on-chain — and start connecting them.”

**Picture:** 00:00–00:05: full-screen still of a selected Xtrata artwork, with a very slow push-in. Label: “ART”. 00:05–00:10: recorded footage of a working music inscription; show the playhead moving. Label: “MUSIC”. 00:10–00:15: recorded X Chess replay, showing one move. Label: “APPS”. 00:15–00:20: reduce those three images into cards; animate thin lines connecting them and reveal the Xtrata logo.

**Sound:** Quiet instrumental pulse underneath. Let a short excerpt from the selected track rise during the gap after “A song”, then lower it beneath the voice. No overlapping music beds during that excerpt. Leave the final two seconds for the logo to settle.

### 00:20–00:50 — Welcome

**Jim, on camera:**

“Gm everyone, I’m Jim. Welcome to the Xtrata Bootcamp, and thank you to TheBigMac and BFF ARMY for bringing us together.

Whether you’re an artist, a musician, a developer, or simply curious, this session is for you. You don’t need to know how to code.

We’ll start with the basics, then work our way towards music, collections, and applications you can actually use.”

**Picture:** Cut to Jim full-screen. Lower third: “Jim | Xtrata Bootcamp”. At about 00:35, cut to a still title card with Jim in a small picture-in-picture window: “XTRATA BOOTCAMP” / “From your first inscription to music and apps”. Small credit: “Hosted by BFF ARMY”.

**Sound:** Fade the opening music out over the first three seconds. Clean microphone audio from here.

### 00:50–01:30 — Why this is interesting

**Jim, voiceover:**

“Most of us are used to uploading our work to a platform. We share a link, and that platform becomes the place people go to find it.

But websites change. Links break. Services close.

Xtrata takes a different approach: the content itself can be stored on-chain, and software can read it back and reconstruct it.

That gives us something interesting to explore: a work that other tools can find, display, and build around.”

**Picture:** Begin with a clean illustrative still: a file card and a browser window connected by a link. Briefly fade the browser window; do not show another company's logo. At “stored on-chain”, transition into a conceptual animation: file → ordered data blocks → reassembled file. Label: “Store → Read → Reconstruct”. Finish with the same reconstructed artwork in two generic viewer frames.

**Sound:** Voice only. Allow a two-second pause after “Services close” and a short hold on the reconstructed file.

**Accuracy note:** This is a conceptual diagram, not a literal transaction trace or a claim that interfaces never need maintenance.

### 01:30–02:10 — What Xtrata is

**Jim, voiceover, returning to camera at the end:**

“Xtrata is an on-chain data protocol built on Stacks, which connects this activity to Bitcoin.

The files we’re talking about are stored on Stacks. They aren’t being written directly into Bitcoin blocks.

You’ll hear me use the word ‘inscription’. For today, think of that as a piece of content recorded on-chain, with an identity that lets us find it again.

That content might be an image, some text, a piece of music, or the code for an application.”

**Picture:** Animate a restrained three-part diagram: “Your work” above “Xtrata • data protocol on Stacks”; “Bitcoin” below, connected with a line labelled “Stacks settlement connection”. Hold the completed diagram. Transition to four still cards: image, text, audio, HTML. On “identity”, reveal an ID beneath a real selected inscription screenshot. Use its actual ID only after confirming the asset.

**Sound:** Voice only. Give the distinction about Stacks and Bitcoin room to land.

### 02:10–02:55 — Music preview

**Jim, voiceover:**

“Music is a good place to see this become real.

Later, we’ll use SUNO More to take a prepared track, review its artwork and details, and turn it into an on-chain player.

We’ll look at what you’re uploading, what it costs, and how someone else can open it and listen.

Here’s a quick taste of the finished experience.

[Pause for six seconds of music.]

And the wider idea goes beyond a single song: artwork, credits, and other connected pieces can become part of the experience.”

**Picture:** Recorded footage: a prepared MP3 entering SUNO More, then a readable hold on artwork/title/artist. Cut to a previously completed player, with a small caption “Completed example”. At “quick taste”, show the player running full-screen for six seconds. Finish on a still pairing the artwork and player.

**Sound:** Use the audio from the demonstrated track for the six-second listening window. Fade down before Jim resumes. Use a track Jim owns or has permission to include in the replay and promotional edits.

**Accuracy note:** Do not imply that the upload completed in the duration of the montage. The input workflow and finished result are separate shots.

### 02:55–03:40 — From objects to applications

**Jim, voiceover:**

“Then we’ll look at what happens when inscriptions refer to other inscriptions.

You’ll hear that called ‘recursion’. The practical idea is simple: an existing piece can become a building block for something else.

An application might refer to artwork, sounds, or code that already exists.

We’ll explore that idea through collections and working applications, including X Chess.

The browser runs the application; we’ll look at how its on-chain content and any supporting contracts fit together. You don’t need to understand the machinery yet. First, we’ll show you what it does.”

**Picture:** Conceptual animation: three cards labelled “Artwork”, “Sound”, “Code”, with arrows flowing into a larger card labelled “Application”. Keep the original cards visible to convey reference and reuse. Cut to 10–12 seconds of recorded X Chess: open a known working game or replay, show a move, then hold. If a second verified app is ready, show it briefly; otherwise stay on the board.

**Sound:** Voice only; optional very quiet chess move sound during a speech gap.

**Accuracy note:** Keep the generic dependency diagram visually separate from X Chess footage. It must not imply that this exact app uses every illustrated asset type. Label replay footage “Recorded replay”; never imply that replaying a move submits a live transaction.

### 03:40–04:20 — The route through the bootcamp

**Jim, voiceover:**

“We’ve split the bootcamp into five chapters.

First, what Xtrata is and how it works.

Second, preparing and inscribing your first file.

Third, music and SUNO More.

Fourth, art, collections, and connected inscriptions.

And fifth, applications, with X Chess and other examples.

Each chapter should work on its own when you come back to the recording. By the end, you should have a clear idea of what you’d like to explore or make yourself.”

**Picture:** Animated chapter list. Reveal one line per spoken chapter, then hold the whole list. Use five restrained thumbnails from earlier shots; no new examples. Keep Jim in a small camera window if the visual remains readable.

**On-screen copy:** “01 Understand / 02 Inscribe / 03 Music / 04 Collections / 05 Applications”. Supporting title: “Five chapters. Start with the basics.”

**Sound:** Voice only. Allow brief pauses between chapters so the text can be read.

### 04:20–05:00 — Participation and handoff

**Jim, on camera:**

“If you’re here live, put your questions in the chat. We’ll pick them up between chapters, so we can keep the demonstrations moving.

You’re welcome to just watch today. When we reach the practical section, I’ll explain what you need before you try anything yourself.

And if you’re watching the replay, welcome — you can pause, revisit a chapter, and follow the example links alongside the video.

As we go, keep one question in mind: what would you put on-chain?

Let’s start with the basics. What is Xtrata?”

**Picture:** Jim full-screen, then a still reading “What would you put on-chain?” over the three opening examples. Return to Jim for the final line. At 04:56, dissolve to “01 / What is Xtrata?” and hold to 05:00. Chapter one begins immediately afterward.

**Sound:** Voice only. Optional short, soft transition tone as the chapter card appears, after the last spoken word.

## Recording and playback approach

Recommended approach: prerecord this five-minute opening with Jim's camera and voice, then play it at the beginning of the live session and switch to Jim live at 05:00. This fixes the timing, preserves the music mix and creates a clean opening for the replay. A short host welcome can happen before the timed programme begins.

For live delivery instead, prepare these eight visual cues as separate scenes. An operator changes scenes at Jim's spoken cues; visual loops or still holds absorb timing differences. Avoid an unattended five-minute visual reel underneath a live reading because even small pacing differences will accumulate.

| Cue | Trigger | Operator action |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Programme starts | Start opening montage and music |
| Q2 | “Gm everyone” | Jim camera, lower third, fade bed |
| Q3 | “Most of us” | File/link illustration and reconstruction animation |
| Q4 | “Xtrata is” | Protocol relationship diagram |
| Q5 | “Music is a good place” | SUNO More footage; music excerpt on “quick taste” |
| Q6 | “Then we’ll look” | Dependency animation, then recorded chess replay |
| Q7 | “We’ve split” | Chapter list |
| Q8 | “If you’re here live” | Jim camera, question card, chapter-one title |

Record a local master. Test a 30-second sample through the actual meeting/stream path and listen back to the saved recording: speech, shared music and picture must all arrive. Keep the camera microphone from picking up the playback through speakers by monitoring on headphones. Record clean narration separately if convenient for later edits. Add captions to the edited recording; do not bake transient meeting chat into the visual master.

## Editing and rehearsal notes

- Speak warmly at roughly 130–140 words per minute during voiced sections; use the planned holds rather than stretching every sentence.
- Run one timed rehearsal and adjust holds to land the chapter card at 04:56 and the handoff at 05:00.
- Keep on-screen labels large enough to read in a meeting window. Zoom or crop screen captures to the relevant controls.
- Use direct cuts and short dissolves. Let explanatory animations settle before moving on.
- Do not use pending transactions, loading screens or unverified prototypes as the opening showcase.
- Keep speculative possibilities distinct from demonstrated functionality.
- Pair the published recording with the five chapter links, example links and creator credits promised by the script.
