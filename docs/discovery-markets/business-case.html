Streams can be botted. Playlists can be bought. Charts can be gamed. There is no trustworthy measure anywhere in this industry of whether a record actually connected with anybody. We think that single missing number is a company.

02  ·  The idea

Run the measurement nobody runs

In 2006 Salganik, Dodds and Watts put 48 unknown songs in front of 14,341 people, split across eight identical copies of the same website running at the same time. Crucially they also ran a control group who could not see any download counts at all.

So every song was measured twice. Once with the crowd visible, and once with the crowd hidden. The paper treats the second number, the one with no social signal in it, as the song's actual quality.

Streaming only ever runs the first measurement. Nobody has ever run the second one as a product.

That is what we are building. A record is played to a cohort of listeners drawn at random, who never saw a price, never saw a forecast, do not follow the artist, and are never told they are the ones being measured. What that cohort does next is the number.

It is unreachable by design. To move it you would need to control roughly a tenth of the entire listener base, and you would never learn which tenth you needed, because the cohort is drawn after forecasting closes and is never published. That is the difference between a number anyone can buy and a number nobody can.

03  ·  Timing

The demand arrived three weeks ago, in public, with a price tag.

Every prediction market wants music. None of them can settle it.

Kalshi and Polymarket are together worth around $37bn. Both already run music and entertainment markets. Both settle them on chart and stream data, which is the one thing in music that a determined trader can reach out and change.

In June, Kalshi ran a contract on the most streamed song in the US that month, with around $3m traded. Streams for one record jumped roughly 70% in a day and it went to number one. Kalshi settled and paid out. Spotify then removed over 500,000 streams it did not believe came from real listeners and the track fell to fourth. There is no suggestion the artist or his team was involved. A Kalshi trader is who flagged it.

That is not a scandal, it is a product gap. A market cannot settle on a number its own traders can manufacture. We make the number they need.

04  ·  Who pays

One measurement, four customers

The same held-out cohort number is the product in every case. Only the wrapper changes.

First revenue  ·  smallest build

The prediction markets

Sell Kalshi and Polymarket the settlement data. They keep the exchange, the licence and the liquidity. We supply the only music number their traders cannot reach. This needs no consumer scale at all to start being worth money, and it puts the regulated surface on somebody who already has it.

Near term

Labels and A&R

Early signal, which they already buy through flat paid panels like SoundOut and HitPredictor. Ours is reputation-weighted rather than flat, which is a narrow and testable claim: more signal per listener-hour than a panel of strangers.

The instrument

Listeners

A discovery product where you hear records blind, back them before anyone knows what they are, and build a permanent public record of being early. This is what generates the measurement, so it is both the product and the factory.

Later

Artists

Audience intelligence that is not a play count. Who stayed to the end, who came back a week later unprompted, and which scene actually responded, which is regularly not the one they thought they were in.

05  ·  Money

Real stakes, without selling the chart

Money makes a forecast honest, and it is legal to do this properly in the US, where Polymarket now operates as a CFTC-regulated exchange and event contracts are treated as derivatives rather than as bets. A June 2026 CFTC proposal points further that way, explicitly excluding awards contests from the gaming prohibition because they turn on external judgment.

It is not legal here. Polymarket blocks the UK entirely, the Gambling Commission treats prediction markets as betting intermediaries needing a licence, and the FCA binary options ban is a separate obstacle a gambling licence does not solve. So the UK edition runs on free credits and the paid edition runs where it can.

There is a real design problem underneath that, and it matters more than the regulation. Capital moves price. If the market ranks the records, then whoever has the most money buys the chart, which is pay-to-play wearing a better suit. So the two are separated permanently.

06  ·  Risks

We would rather lose you here than at diligence.

What could kill it

Nobody listens blind

The whole thing rests on people voluntarily hearing unknown music all the way through. That is work, and most people do not want work. This is the top risk, ahead of manipulation, and it is the first thing we test.

Good taste may not be a real thing

Meaning it may be luck that looks like skill in hindsight rather than a repeatable trait. If forecasting skill does not persist across a split-half test, there are no tastemakers and the reputation layer is fiction. Testable, and tested early.

The crowd may not beat the algorithm

Every market opens at a machine prediction and humans only score by beating it, so this resolves to one number. If it is not reliably positive, there is no company, and we would like to be the ones who find that out.

Thin markets

Liquidity is the hard part of this sector and our questions have less natural interest than an election. Markets have to be scarce and earned rather than one per track, and that constrains how fast this scales.

Whoever turns up defines the chart

Early users will skew, and the output will reflect that while sounding objective. Naming whose opinion a chart represents is the minimum. Fixing it is slow, expensive recruitment and no mechanism does it for us.

07  ·  First move

The cheap question, before anything else

Not the market. Not the technology. A two week test of whether anyone will sit and listen to unknown music properly, more than once. A page, a queue of unreleased tracks, no scores and no predictions. We measure completion, depth and seven day return.

If that fails, this goes in a drawer and it costs almost nothing to find out. If it holds, the next build is the cohort mechanism, because the cohort mechanism is the asset. It is what Kalshi visibly lacked in June, and it is the only part of this that cannot be bought with a bigger balance sheet.
