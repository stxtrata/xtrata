# The 30 second version

For saying out loud to people who already work in music.

---

## Say this

> You see the Kalshi thing last month? People betting on Spotify's chart, and then
> half a million fake streams turned up to win the bet.
>
> That's a prediction market watching our industry from the outside.
>
> So flip it. Make the market the A&R.
>
> You hear a track with the name stripped off. You call how far it goes. You only
> score if you beat what the algorithm already guessed. Get it right enough times
> and your calls start moving what everyone else hears.
>
> It's Polymarket, pointed at taste instead of pointed at us.

75 words. About thirty seconds at normal speed. Stop there and let them ask.

---

## If you only get one line

> Polymarket for A&R. You back records before anyone knows what they are, and you
> only score by beating the algorithm.

---

## The two facts to have ready

**The one that makes them lean in.** In 2006 a Science experiment put 48 unknown
songs in front of 14,341 people, split across eight identical copies of the same
site running at once. One song, Lockdown by 52metro, came first out of 48 in one
copy and fortieth in another. Same song, same competition. The only difference was
whether you could see what everyone else had downloaded.

**The one that makes them angry.** 106,000 tracks a day went up last year. 88% of
everything on streaming got under a thousand plays.

---

## What they will ask, and the answer

**"So it's gambling."**
No money in, no money out. Credits are free and you cannot buy more. It is a
rating, closer to Elo in chess than to a bet.

**"What does the artist get?"**
A genuinely blind first listen, which most of them have never had. Then real
numbers. Who stayed to the end, who came back a week later, which scene actually
bit. Not a play count.

**"How do you stop people rigging it?"**
The people who forecast never count towards the result. A separate random group
settles it, picked after the fact, and they are not told they are the ones being
measured. You would need about a tenth of the whole platform to move one record.

**"Why would a crowd beat our A&R?"**
Maybe it will not. That is the first thing we test, and we will publish it either
way. But the algorithm goes first and humans only score by beating it, so we find
out very quickly whether there is anything there.

**"Are you trying to be Spotify?"**
No. That is a licensing war nobody wins from nothing. We want to be the thing that
finds records first. If that works, Spotify and the labels are customers.

## Do not say

- Blockchain, wallet, token, inscription. None of it is consumer facing and it will
  cost you the room.
- Any number you have not checked. Everything above is sourced on the web page.
- That it exists. It does not. It is a design and a two week test that has not
  been run.
