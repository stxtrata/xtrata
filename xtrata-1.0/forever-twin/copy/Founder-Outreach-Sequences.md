Draft status: internal use. Do not accuse specific collections of risk without evidence.

## Stacks Collection Founder Version

Subject/DM opener: Quick permanence check for your collection

Your collection may be fine today, but if the media path breaks, holders can be left with valid tokens pointing at nothing.

I am not saying your collection is broken. I am saying this is becoming a visible infrastructure risk for NFT projects generally: gateways change, marketplaces shut down, metadata endpoints move, pinning accounts lapse.

Bitcoin Pepes are already live as the first public Forever Twins collection, powered by Xtrata and built with Fak.fun. Holders have started claiming on Fak.fun, with around 200 Pepes added to Xtrata so far.

The model gives an existing NFT a permanent on-chain counterpart without pretending the original collection never existed.

Would you like us to run a free collection permanence audit and show where your media and metadata dependencies are?

## Music NFT Version

Subject: Preserving the actual audio, not just the token

Music NFTs have a sharper version of the same problem: the token may survive while the audio, cover art, metadata, or unlockable experience depends on a media path somewhere else.

Xtrata can store full media bytes on-chain, including larger files through chunked uploads. The local v3.2.3 contract source sets a 32 MiB hard cap, but live deployment and product flow should be confirmed before quoting limits publicly.

Forever Twins can give existing music NFTs an on-chain counterpart while preserving the original collection.

Would you like a collection permanence audit?

## Follow-Up 1

Following up on the permanence audit idea.

The useful output is simple:

- where the collection media lives;
- what the token actually points to;
- which gateway/server/metadata paths are critical;
- whether the original contract can update anything;
- what a Forever Twin or repointing flow would look like.

No public claims. No accusation that anything is broken.

Just a clear infrastructure map before something breaks in public.

## Follow-Up 2

Last follow-up from me.

The reason I think this matters: a temporary media failure is often invisible to holders because marketplaces or indexers can quietly repair display.

That is good in the short term, but it can hide the real risk.

If the person, account, gateway, marketplace, or backup is gone next time, temporary failure becomes permanent loss.

Happy to run the audit if useful.
