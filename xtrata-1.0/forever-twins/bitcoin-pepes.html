The mechanic

How forever twins work

No re-minting. No migration. Your original Pepe is never destroyed — it gains a permanent, on-chain twin that the two of you can never be separated from.

1

Inscribe

You call inscribe on your Pepe. Xtrata writes a permanent, fully on-chain copy of the artwork and binds a forever twin to your token 1:1.

2

One is always locked

At any moment you hold exactly one of the pair; the contract escrows the other. swap-pepe-for-xtrata and swap-xtrata-for-pepe flip which one you hold — they can never both be loose, and never be split apart.

3

Finalized forever

Each twin carries a canonical content hash. Once the collection is finalized, that record is locked permanently — no one, not even the contract owner, can alter it.

Why this matters

"Permanent" ownership has a dirty secret

Most NFTs don't contain their art — they point to a file hosted somewhere off-chain. When that host stops paying the bill, the art disappears. This isn't theoretical; it's measured, and it has already happened to blue-chip collections.

~20%

of NFTs are already broken — dead links, missing images, or IPFS that no longer resolves — across scans of ~498,000 and 400,000+ tokens.

98

of the top 1,000 collections by volume already point at URLs that no longer exist. 277 sit entirely on centralized servers.

$1.5M

of Coachella NFTs went to blank gray boxes the moment FTX's domain was redirected after bankruptcy.

FTX / Coachella

Lifetime-pass NFTs broke instantly when the FTX.us domain went to a restructuring page. Ownership intact; the asset, gone.

nft.storage Classic

Data was safe on Filecoin, but millions of contracts hardcoded the dead gateway. Art vanished from wallets anyway.

CloneX / RTFKT (Nike)

~19,000 NFTs replaced with a Cloudflare "terms violation" message after a downgrade to a free plan (Apr 2025).

Infura gateway (2022)

Thousands of tokens bricked overnight when one hardcoded gateway URL was deprecated.

Backups (pinning services, Arweave copies) keep data alive but don't fix the original token's pointer. A forever twin gives you a second, permanent token you actually own — independent of anyone's hosting bill.

Questions

FAQ

Do I lose my original Pepe?

No. Your Pepe is never burned or altered. Inscribing creates a second token — the forever twin — bound to it. One of the pair always sits in the contract; you can swap which one you hold at any time, but they can never be separated.

Why are there two tokens?

The original Pepe lives on a contract whose art could rot. The forever twin is a permanent, fully on-chain copy on Xtrata. Keeping them bound 1:1 — with one always escrowed — means the permanent record travels with the collectible as a single economic unit.

What makes it "forever"?

Each twin stores a canonical content hash and the artwork itself on-chain via Xtrata. Once the collection's canonical records are finalized, they're immutable — no one can change or remove them, including the team. The live "Canonical record" stat above shows the current state.

How much does it cost?

The first 87 inscriptions are completely free. After that it's a flat 3 STX inscribe fee (plus the small Xtrata storage fee for writing your art on-chain). The live counter above shows how many free spots remain.

I run another collection — can I do this?

Yes. The forever-twin model is designed to repoint existing collections without re-minting or migrating holders. Bitcoin Pepes is the flagship; the same pattern works for any Stacks collection. Reach out about a repointing audit.
