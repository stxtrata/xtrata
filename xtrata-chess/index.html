Board · no game

Pick a piece, then pick where you want it to go.

You can also type a move in UCI, the notation chess programs use between themselves. It is just the square a piece starts on followed by the square it ends on, so e2e4 means "the piece on e2 goes to e4". When a pawn reaches the far end and becomes a new piece, add a letter for it: e7e8q promotes to a queen. Nothing is sent anywhere until you press Submit move.

Start your own game, with your own rules
Who may move the white pieces. Type “anyone” to leave it open to everybody, “anyone-else” for everybody except whoever plays black, or a wallet address or .btc name to keep it to one person.
Who may move the black pieces. The same choices as white. One name on one side and “anyone-else” on the other makes a game of one player against everybody.
Stops the same person playing two moves in a row, so nobody can answer their own move. Separate from the wait above: both are applied, and ticking this is the same as setting the wait to 1. After somebody moves, how many other moves must be played before that same person may move again. 0 lets anyone move whenever they like. 1 is the same as ticking “nobody can move twice in a row”. 3 means you move, then wait for three other moves. Counted in moves, not in time, so it works the same whether the game takes an hour or a year. A wait of N needs N+1 different people able to move, or the game locks up.

hash of these rules

The blockchain does not check any of these rules. When the game starts it saves only a hash of them: a short fingerprint that could not have come from any other set of rules. The page you download is the referee. It reads every move, ignores the ones that break your rules, and checks its own rules against that hash, so it can prove it is refereeing the game it claims to be. Inscribe that page and your game gets what this one has: a permanent front end, permanently tied to a live contract. Start the game first, then download its page.

This page is an inscription. It is not hosted anywhere and nobody can update it: these exact bytes are written into the Stacks blockchain, and they will still be there long after any website has gone. What you have been watching is that permanent page reading a live smart contract — the board changes as people play, while the thing drawing it never changes at all.
