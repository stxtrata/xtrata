No first place and no runner-up. The pool splits evenly, because ranking creative work across five different mediums was never going to be honest. Each of these is permanent, and each one uses the chain for something the chain is actually good at.

2897 Token

Keeper of Vanishing Words

Interactive HTML 31.7 KB 28 July SP3PF8P0B6…288TJGTD4

Six words from languages that are endangered, critically endangered, or almost gone. Each one really is spoken aloud, by the browser's own voice, imperfectly, the way any voice learning a word for the first time does. No server is involved in that, which is the point. The argument underneath is the best case for this medium anyone made all month. For as long as language has existed a word has needed a living mouth to survive, and the moment nobody is left to say it, it goes quiet. For the first time a word can be carried by something that never dies of old age.

A Letter, Sealed

Interactive HTML 11.4 KB 28 July SPPC6F6C2M…67JE06J4

A letter written to a nine month old son, presented as a document waiting to be opened. The reasoning is what earns it a place here. Memories get kinder to themselves with age, so rather than leave a story that will soften, the writer left a fact that can be checked, unchanged. The wax seal is drawn rather than enforced, and deliberately so. How you actually seal something until a date arrives is left open, because there are many ways it might be done and the good ones have probably not been invented yet. The piece states the wish and leaves the method to whoever solves it. What is permanent today is the letter, which can no longer be quietly edited or taken down.

What actually happened

Nobody submitted a pitch. They submitted proof.

That was the whole idea, and it held. There were no decks and no roadmaps. People made a thing, put it somewhere it cannot be edited, and said why. Here is what the chain now holds.

01 / The page is the artwork

Ten self-contained web pieces

The largest single category was not images. It was HTML. Ten entries were complete interactive works with their own canvas animation, their own audio, their own typography, no external request of any kind. One of them declared in its own footer that it had been kept deliberately light so it could genuinely live on-chain rather than just describe living on-chain. That is the medium finding its own shape.

02 / Small work counts

From 25 bytes to 1.76 megabytes

The smallest entry was twenty five bytes long. The largest was seventy thousand times bigger. Both are equally permanent and both cost less than a coffee. Six creators chose plain text and nothing else, which turns out to be the purest form of the thing. Words, sealed, no wrapper.

03 / Formats nobody expected

PDF, SVG, and hand-written vector

Alongside the images and the essays we got a PDF of a typeset essay, and an original SVG illustration written as vector source rather than exported as pixels. A blossom tree above ground with a hexagonal block lattice for roots. Sixty kilobytes, infinitely scalable, and it will render identically in a hundred years.

The most useful surprise

Half the entries designed a product we have not built.

Something worth saying plainly. Several of the strongest entries are complete artworks that contain an imagined interface. A button that says seal this letter. A field that says write one true sentence. A prompt that asks which word you would not want lost. Press them and nothing leaves the page, because none of these works connect to a wallet or a contract. They were never meant to.

We could treat that as a gap. We would rather treat it as a specification. When creators are handed a permanent medium and asked what they want, and they independently draw the same three controls, that is not a mistake in their work. That is a roadmap arriving unprompted, drawn by the people who would use it.

Requested / 01

Let a reader add to a work

Three separate entries built an input box inviting the visitor to contribute a sentence, a word, or a memory. Nobody could wire it up, because a page cannot mint on someone else's behalf today. A child inscription written by a reader and pointing at the parent is exactly what recursion is for, and it is the single most requested thing in the whole campaign.

Requested / 02

A letter that opens on a date

The most affecting entry is built entirely around a lock that does not exist yet. A sealed message with a stated opening date, unreadable until then. The entrant did not propose a mechanism, and was right not to. Encryption with a released key, a covenant, a delegated opener, a social threshold. There are many routes and the good one may not be invented yet. The demand is now on record, written by somebody with a reason.

Requested / 03

Show me my own proof

More than one entry drew its own receipt, a small panel stating what was inscribed and where it lives. The Wizard already writes exactly that as a recursive child of every job it completes. Creators clearly want it visible inside the artwork, not filed away beside it, and that is a much smaller ask than it sounds.

Open ground

Two whole territories are still empty.

This is the most useful thing the campaign told us, and we are saying it plainly rather than quietly. Two of the most powerful things Xtrata can do went almost entirely unclaimed. If you are looking for where to plant a flag, it is right here.

Unclaimed / 01

Nobody inscribed music.

Not one entrant put a track on-chain. The path is built and proven. A single self-contained HTML player, embedded Opus audio, cover art, transport controls, the whole thing sealed as one object that plays in any browser with no streaming service behind it. We ran multi-megabyte songs through it during the campaign and they work. The first person to inscribe a real original track through Xtrata will be the first person to have done it. That is a one-time-only position.

Unclaimed / 02

Almost nobody used recursion.

Every entry stood alone. Xtrata objects can name parents, declare dependencies, and build on inscriptions that already exist, including inscriptions made by other people. During the whole campaign the only recursive objects created were the receipts the Wizard writes automatically, each one a child that points at the work it paid for. Not a single entrant reached for it on purpose. Remix, response, sequel, annotation, a cover version that cites the original as its parent. All of it is sitting there unused.

The door did not close

The bounty ended. The archive did not.

Nothing about inscribing depended on the prize. A single file up to 512 KB still mints in one transaction on the Create page, and anything larger, or a folder, or a whole collection, still goes through the guided Wizard. It costs less than you think and it lasts longer than anything else you will publish this year.

Make something. Seal it. Tell people why. If it is music or if it builds on somebody else's work, you will be first.
