XTRATA · BIP-110 & the future of on-chain media

What BIP-110 meant for on-chain media — honestly

The short version: BIP-110 was a proposed Bitcoin rule change that would have made storing data on Bitcoin L1 much harder: data pushes capped at 256 bytes, and the script trick behind today's Ordinals envelope disabled. It did not activate. Its mandatory signalling window opened on 8 August 2026 with almost no miner support, a small minority chain stalled after two blocks, and Bitcoin carried on without it. Nothing already inscribed was ever at risk: existing inscriptions live in blocks that are permanent history.

The question it raised is still a good one

BIP-110 failed, but the worry behind it was real. Every byte written to Bitcoin is carried by every node, for ever, on behalf of everyone who comes after, and anyone with enough money for fees can fill blocks with whatever they like. The ord developers drafted an envelope that would have worked within BIP-110's limits (ord PR #4545), and the BIP-110 text itself conceded that data can only be made harder and more expensive to store, not stopped. Where Xtrata stands

We think Bitcoin Core's instinct is defensible: L1 is for money. That's a big part of why Xtrata exists: full media files, recursive references and composable records on a layer built for them, with every transaction anchored to Bitcoin's finality through Stacks. That isn't a licence to store everything. Stacks block space is shared too, so we compress what we inscribe, reuse existing inscriptions instead of uploading them again, and are exploring storage chains such as Arweave for the heaviest media, with the fingerprint and the record that ties each file to its token kept on Stacks.

We come from the Ordinals world ourselves (Xtrata's founder built Audionals on Bitcoin L1, squeezing whole songs into a few kilobytes), and small, carefully made work still has a place there. We'd just rather make every byte count, on either layer, than look for ways around the people asking us to use fewer of them.

Does my existing Ordinals work disappear?

No. Consensus changes constrain new transactions only. Everything already inscribed is inside blocks protected by all the proof-of-work mined since — archival nodes carry it forever, and any new node syncing the chain verifies it byte-for-byte. The realistic long-term risk isn't deletion; it's tooling: fewer indexers and viewers being maintained if inscribing declines. Preserving access infrastructure is precisely the layer Xtrata builds — including Forever Twins, permanent counterparts for existing collections.
