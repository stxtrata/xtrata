## Binance 2026 (still unfolding)

Cards say "as reported, 2 November 2026". Sources agree on 29 October 2026, about $6.3B (TRM Labs $6.36B, Elliptic $6.43B), ten blockchains, about $3B frozen (Elliptic verified $2.85B
frozen or seized), withdrawals reopened 1 November, and "Binance will absorb the financial impact of these stolen funds, not our users." Replenished: about 90% on 31 Oct (Wikipedia),
about 95% by 2 Nov (The Register). Attribution to the FBI-named group is by the FBI and TRM Labs; Wikipedia still words it as "allegedly": the game keeps "alleged". Disputed or unsettled:
Binance first blamed a software engineer's compromised laptop and "other issues were later found"; the description of EIP-7702 as "multi-signature" looks like a mislabel; the roughly 20-minute
timeline is TRM Labs'. Proof of reserves was promised for 2 November.

## Could not be verified or deliberately left out

* Pages that could not be fetched: NPR and CNBC (robots/error), BleepingComputer's Binance piece (error), CBC (error), Wikipedia pages for Zaif, DMM Bitcoin, "2025 Bybit hack" and the exchange-hack list.
  NPR and CNBC are listed as `listing` sources only.
* No USD figure for Mt. Gox 2014 is stated in any fetched source, so none is shown (the 7% share is used).
* Whether Coincheck's coins were in a hot wallet without multi-sig, and Bybit replacing the stolen funds, are widely reported but not in the fetched text: left off the cards.
* How the Bitfinex signing was bypassed, and the cause of Mt. Gox's losses, are not explained in the fetched sources: the cards say so rather than guess.
* Upbit's "hot wallet" detail, Bybit's "Safe{Wallet}" as Bybit's own first account, and the Bitstamp day (4 January) rest on a single source each; the Bybit headquarters is Dubai (Wikipedia).
* Proof of reserves has no reachable reference page: the glossary line is a plain definition, flagged in `facts.json`.
* `WITHDRAWAL LIMITS` and `ADDRESS ALLOW-LISTS` lessons from the brief were left out: no fetched source ties either to a specific incident. 14 lessons are included instead, each tied to a sourced incident.
* the FBI-named group: the Wikipedia Lazarus page does not use the name; the FBI IC3 alert, BleepingComputer (DMM) and The Register (Binance) do, so the glossary line says only "the name the FBI uses".
