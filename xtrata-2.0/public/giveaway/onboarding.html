Why this matters

Web3 music assets, handed to people who don’t know or care what a wallet is. Most of the industry’s attempts stop at a wallet-connect button. This one starts from a link in a message.

And a player that pays artists as it plays: every song heard for 30 seconds sends a small payment straight to the holder of the song.

A clean test

So the test is fair

  • Copies go one at a time. If the live copy has just been claimed, the drop page counts down to the next one. Tell them to keep it open.
  • One copy of each album per person. It’s tied to their passkey, so it’s their copy, not one you can claim on their behalf.
  • If it looks stuck, open the link in Safari or Chrome rather than inside a chat app’s built-in browser.
  • You can go first. Already on Stacks? You can also connect Xverse or Leather on the radio and listen with your own wallet.

Under the hood

What actually happens

  • Songs are inscribed on Xtrata on Stacks. Each paid play is a small STX transfer to the song’s holder, with the network fee on top.
  • The listening wallet is a Stacks wallet derived from a passkey (Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello). The key never leaves the device.
  • Claiming a link sends a starter balance to a brand-new wallet. Each album goes into its own numbered wallet under the same passkey, and only one copy of an album per passkey.
  • The giveaway hands out one link at a time, with a ten minute pause between copies so a crowd never turns into a rush.
