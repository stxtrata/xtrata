Cross-Chain Preservation Census

A targeted look at NFT collections on Ethereum, Base, Polygon, Arbitrum and Tezos, hunting for art at risk. __NTRI__ collections were screened for weak links, and the __NDEEP__ riskiest were tested token by token, once through the contract's own link and once through an indexer's cached copy.

Two ways to find the art

Layer 1 · the contract
contract→tokenURI→metadata file→image

The route that belongs to the token itself. If any link in it is gone, the NFT no longer points at its art.

Layer 2 · the indexer copy
Alchemy / TzKT→cached metadata→cached image

Indexers keep their own copy of what they last saw. When layer 1 dies, wallets and marketplaces often keep showing the art from here, so a dead collection can look alive.

How this was measured

  • Candidates: CoinGecko's top 500 NFT collections by market cap (EVM chains), objkt.com's top 100 Tezos collections by volume, and 11 hand-picked platform contracts (Foundation, Zora, SuperRare, KnownOrigin, hic et nunc and others).
  • Screening: each contract's code was read for an upgradeable proxy, URI-setter functions and a freeze function, and two token pointers were classified. Collections scoring 3 or more (an IPFS gateway, a web server or bucket, an app host), plus the hand-picked set, went to the deep test.
  • Deep test: about six tokens each, metadata and image fetched as written, then through public IPFS gateways; the indexer copy (Alchemy for EVM, TzKT for Tezos) loaded separately; IPFS routing asked who still stores each folder.
  • Dead hosts were hand-checked: a DNS lookup for every dead pointer host, and a server-side fetch where the host allowed one.
  • This is a sample, not every token, from one run on one day and one network. Re-run before naming anything as lost.
  • "Fragile" is about single points of failure (one web server, one bucket, one IPFS provider), not about the team behind a collection.
  • A server that serves the art today can change what it serves tomorrow. The census records reachability, not whether the bytes match the original.
  • Safe collections were screened but mostly not deep-tested, so the deep-test bar over-represents risk by design. On-chain collections (Autoglyphs, Checks, Loot, Terraforms and others) scored 0 and were not deep-tested.
  • Items marked "(verify)" in the notes rest on one signal and need a second check.
