of a smaller first milestone in September. That was a mistake: offering a cheaper option
alongside the real one invites them to take the cheaper one, and it concedes three weeks
before anyone has pushed back. If they decline, negotiate then.

The ask needs no date. Their own page sets the trigger, "released upon blueprint
approval", and the blueprint is done bar two definitions. So the reply settles those
definitions in the same message, which removes the last thing standing between here and
that payment.

Supporting facts, both verified:
- The grant page has shown the Initial Payment row since the grant went active.
- The 4 August email says "Once a milestone is approved, the corresponding payment will
  be released." That is the same mechanism the row describes, so the two agree.

Attach the milestones-tab screenshot. It is the whole basis of the ask, and the DeGrants
team are working from their own contracting template, so the grantee-facing view may not
be something anyone on their side has looked at. Frame it as help, not as evidence.

Honest limit: no agreement is signed, so this is fair dealing rather than an enforceable
term. Also from the 4 August email, nothing public until the agreement is signed.

**The 25 wallets.** A Forever Twin is minted into the collection's escrow contract and
bound to the wallet that owns the original. The two are attached. If you own the original
the twin is yours, and that is what the registry shows and how all three live collections
work today.

Counting raw custody instead would mean asking 25 people to deposit their original NFT
into a contract to take the twin out. That is a custody decision, not a preservation
outcome, and it would report the escrow contract as the only holder however much art had
been preserved. So I would like it to read 25 unique wallets with a Forever Twin
resolved to them, and I will report both figures in the recap.

**At-risk.** This describes almost every collection on Stacks, so it needs a test rather
than a judgement. Here is the one I will use and publish:

- Does the contract's token URI still resolve? This is the deterministic part and the
  clearest failure. At least one collection I have checked returns 404 for every token id
  I tried, so its holders have no route from their token to their own artwork.
- Is the metadata frozen, or can the pointer still be repointed later? Most are not
  frozen.
- How hard is the artwork to retrieve, measured across several public gateways rather
  than one, because a single gateway timing out proves nothing about whether the content
  exists.

A collection qualifies as at risk on the first two, which are binary and checkable on
chain. The third is reported as a difficulty score rather than a pass or fail. I will
publish the measurement for every collection surveyed, not just the ones selected, and I
will tell any collection I name before I publish.
