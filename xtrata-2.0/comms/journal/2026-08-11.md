## The one number I deliberately did not inflate

Jim said "people are already playing games". True, but the honest shape of it
is that **two** addresses have opened games, one of which is the deployer.
The other, `SP10W2EEM757922QTVDZZ5CSEW55JEFNN30J69TM7`, opened games 5 and 6.

So no post claims a crowd. The line that went into the queue is "Somebody who
is not me has opened games on it", which is verified, and which happens to
land better than a made-up player count because it feeds the anyone-else
argument about not being able to play both colours.

Nine games and eighty moves are strong enough numbers to lead with. They do not
need help.

## Rejected, and why

- **A player-count post.** Two addresses is not a story yet. Revisit when a
  third party opens a game without being asked.
- **Anything about mobile or passkey.** The hand-written board flags both as
  UNVERIFIED and nothing has changed that. Left out entirely rather than
  queued with a warning.
- **A "first chess game on Bitcoin" style claim.** Not checked, probably not
  true, and the kind of thing that gets replied to with a counterexample.
- **Naming a winner or a finished game.** `get-result-hint` exists but was not
  read, so no result claim is safe yet.
- **A launch post promising the next version.** "Later versions will be their
  own inscriptions" made it in because it is a property of the design. A date
  did not, because there isn't one.

## The image pipeline, added the same day

`npm run comms:images` now gives every post curated, captured and generated
options. 24 of 24 posts have at least one, 43 distinct images in total.

Three things it taught us immediately:

**The move count is not the move count.** `get-game` `next-seq` counts
**submissions**, not accepted moves. The live board says plainly: "4 submissions
did not count. Each was still stored on chain and still cost its sender a
network fee." So 87 is the number of signed transactions, and some of those
were not legal moves. Both posts and the stat card were reworded to say signed
transactions, which is exactly true and makes the better point anyway. This is
now in the registry's `verifyBeforeUse`.

**The number moves fast.** The total was 80 at about 13:00 and 87 by 21:00 on
launch day. A count read the night before a post is wrong by morning. Re-read
it immediately before sending.

**A bare capture is often the wrong screen.** `https://xtrata.xyz/i/2988` opens
the "start a game" form, and the automated shot caught it displaying its own
validation warnings. A `#game=4` hash did not route either. The capture now
drives the UI, filling the game number field and clicking through, and it
produces a real board: game 4, xtrata.btc against SP1C...KXFF6W, mid position,
move list visible. Worth knowing that a third address appears there as a player
even though only two have ever opened a game.
