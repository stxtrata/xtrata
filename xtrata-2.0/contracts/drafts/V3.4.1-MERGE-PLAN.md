# Building v3.4.1: what to take from v3.2.4, what to leave

**Who this is for:** Jim, deciding what goes in. Written to be read without knowing Clarity.

**The plan in one line:** start from v3.4.0, add the small number of things v3.2.4 does
better, fix two defects, ship as v3.4.1.

---

## 1. The situation in plain terms

You have two unshipped contracts. Both start from the same v3.2.3 that is live on mainnet
today. Neither knows about the other, because they were written three weeks apart by
different pieces of work.

| | v3.4.0 | v3.2.4 |
|---|---|---|
| Written | 2 July | 27 July |
| What it is about | A better way to move old inscriptions onto the new contract | Letting one person pay for someone else's inscription |
| Deployed | No | No |

The version numbers are misleading. 3.4.0 is the **older** one despite the higher number.

Neither is a superset of the other, so "pick one" loses something either way. v3.4.1 is the
merge.

---

## 2. The recommendation, at a glance

Start with v3.4.0 as the base. Then:

| | What | From | Why | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | The `-to` mint functions | v3.2.4 | **The whole reason DeOrganized is waiting.** Without this, payer and owner cannot differ | Small |
| **B** | Keep the original creator when migrating | v3.2.4 | v3.4.0 quietly rewrites the creator to whoever migrates. That destroys provenance | Small |
| **C** | Raise the size ceiling to 128 MiB | v3.2.4 | One number. Opens up larger media | Trivial |
| **D** | Fix the version string | neither | v3.4.0 says "3.4.0". It must say "3.4.1" | Trivial |
| **E** | Fee change timelock | new | Turns a promise you have to remember into something the contract enforces | Medium |

And deliberately **do not** take:

| | What | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| **F** | `migrate-from-v3-2-3` and friends | v3.4.0's migration is better in every way. See section 4 |
| **G** | The chunk proxy (`get-migrated-chunk`) | v3.4.0 does not need it. See section 4 |

## 3. Item A: the thing DeOrganized is waiting for

**The problem today.** When someone inscribes, the contract charges the fee to whoever sent
the transaction, and gives the inscription to that same person. Payer and owner are welded
together. There is no way to say "I will pay, but give it to her."

**What v3.2.4 adds.** Three new ways to mint, each a copy of an existing one with a
"recipient" added:

| Existing, unchanged | New version |
|---|---|
| `mint-single-tx` | `mint-single-tx-to` |
| `mint-single-tx-recursive` | `mint-single-tx-recursive-to` |
| `mint-single-tx-with-relationships` | `mint-single-tx-with-relationships-to` |

**Who ends up as what:**

| | Normal mint | The new `-to` mint |
|---|---|---|
| Pays the fee | The sender | The sender |
| Owns the inscription | The sender | **The named recipient** |
| Recorded as the creator | The sender | **The named recipient** |
| Shows up as "payer" in the event log | The sender | The sender |

The recipient becomes both owner **and** creator. For DeOrganized that is better than they
asked for. Their writer gets full on-chain authorship, the platform pays, and the platform
appears only as a footnote in the event log.

**Does it break anything?** No. The three original functions still exist and behave exactly
as they do now. Anyone already integrated notices nothing.

**Any risk of abuse?** Someone could mint an inscription to a stranger's wallet. They would
have to pay for the privilege, so the worst case is an unsolicited gift.

## 4. Items F and G: why v3.4.0's migration wins

This is the part most worth understanding, because the two contracts solve the same problem
in genuinely different ways.

**Migration** means taking an inscription that lives on an old contract and moving it onto
the new one, keeping the same ID number.

### v3.2.4's approach: leave the content behind

It copies the description of the inscription (size, type, hash, link) but **not the actual
content bytes**. When someone later asks the new contract for the content, the new contract
turns around and fetches it from the old contract.

| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| Cheap. 0.1 STX | The new contract permanently depends on the old one |
| Fast | You must write a new function for every old contract you ever want to migrate from, which means a new contract version each time |
| Keeps the original creator | **It is currently broken.** See section 6 |

### v3.4.0's approach: bring the content with you

The person migrating **re-uploads the actual content**, and the contract re-checks the hash
to prove it is the same content. Then it mints at the same ID and takes custody of the old
token.

| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| The content genuinely lives on the new contract. No dependency on the old one | Costs a normal mint fee instead of 0.1 STX |
| Works with **any** old contract, added by you with a single admin call, no redeploy | The person migrating needs a copy of the original content |
| The hash is re-verified, so the content is provably identical | Currently loses the original creator. That is item B |

### Why this matters to you specifically

The v3.2.4 approach is what forces a **new contract version every time you want to support
migrating from somewhere new**. That is the treadmill you are on right now. v3.4.0 replaces
it with an allowlist you control with one transaction.

"The person needs a copy of the original content" sounds like a problem but is not much of
one. The content is readable from the old contract, so it can always be fetched and
resubmitted.

**Verdict: take v3.4.0's migration wholesale. Drop v3.2.4's version entirely.**

## 5. Item B: the provenance bug in v3.4.0

Worth calling out on its own, because it is quiet and it matters.

When v3.4.0 migrates an inscription, it writes the **migrator** in as the creator:

| | Original creator after migration |
|---|---|
| v3.2.4 | Preserved. Whoever actually made it |
| v3.4.0 | **Overwritten** with whoever ran the migration |

If a collector buys an early piece and then migrates it, v3.4.0 records the collector as
having created it. The real artist is erased from the record.

For a protocol whose entire proposition is permanent provenance, that is the wrong default.
It is a small fix: read the creator from the old contract and carry it over, which is what
v3.2.4 already does.

**Verdict: take v3.2.4's handling of the creator field, bolted onto v3.4.0's migration.**

## 6. The two defects to fix

| | Defect | Where | What happens if it ships |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The contract reports the wrong version name | v3.4.0 says `"xtrata-v3.4.0"`. v3.2.4 says `"xtrata-v3.2.3"`, which is simply wrong | Integrators check this string to confirm which contract they are talking to. A contract that lies about its own name breaks that check |
| 2 | v3.2.4's migration cannot read back what it migrated | Its content-fetching helper only knows about v1 and v2. It was never taught about v3.2.3 | Migrate a v3.2.3 inscription and asking for its content returns nothing. The bytes are safe on the old contract, but the new contract will not serve them |

Defect 2 disappears on its own if you follow the recommendation, because you drop v3.2.4's
migration entirely. It is listed so the decision is made knowingly rather than by accident.

## 7. Item E: the fee question you raised

You said a promise you have to remember is the wrong mechanism. Agreed.

**What is already true today, on the live contract**, and worth telling DeOrganized because
nobody has:

| Protection | Value |
|---|---|
| Absolute ceiling on any fee unit | 1 STX. The transaction simply fails above it |
| Most a fee can rise in one change | Double |
| Most a fee can fall in one change | To one tenth |

So the fee cannot be quietly moved to something ruinous. From today's 0.01 STX it would
take seven separate transactions to reach the ceiling.

**What is missing:** a change lands the instant you send it. Nobody can see it coming, and
you cannot take back a mistake.

**Proposed for v3.4.1:**

| Change | Effect |
|---|---|
| Fee **increases** happen in two steps: propose, then confirm after a waiting period | You cannot fat-finger a rise. Integrators get warning |
| Fee **decreases** stay instant | A price cut needs no ceremony |
| A cancel button, and a way to read the pending change | Anyone can ask the contract "is a change coming, and when" |
| Emergency route stays as-is | If something genuinely goes wrong, pause the contract. Do not price-gouge in a hurry |

The waiting period would be counted in Bitcoin blocks, roughly 144 a day, so a week is
about 1008.

The third row is the one that answers DeOrganized's actual question. It makes the notice
period something a machine can read, so it stops depending on you remembering to post.

**This is the only item with real effort attached.** It is fine to ship v3.4.1 without it
and do it in v3.4.2. Your call.
